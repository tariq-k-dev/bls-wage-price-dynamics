# Worksheet Answers to: Is your paycheck keeping up with prices?

### 1. Why is there no CPI for the month you found in Step 2? (Hint: what was happening in Washington?)

- **Answer:** During federal government shutdowns, data collection and publication by agencies like the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) can be disrupted or delayed, which is why historical datasets occasionally include missing value markers (represented as `-` in the raw data stream).   

### 2. Is headline inflation above or below core right now? What does that say about food and energy?

- **Answer:** Headline inflation is **above** core inflation (3.72% vs. 2.57%). Because headline inflation includes food and energy while core inflation excludes them, this indicates that food and energy prices are currently driving the total inflation rate higher than the underlying trend across the rest of the economy.   

### 3. Since September 2025, have wages kept up with prices? What has happened to real wages?

- **Answer:** No, wages have not quite kept up over this specific window. Since September 2025, prices have risen **3.72%** while wages have grown by **3.02%**. Because consumer prices grew faster than nominal earnings, **real wages decreased slightly** over this timeframe.   

### 4. That wage is an average across all workers. Why might your own paycheck tell a different story?

- **Answer:** The BLS wage series (`CES0500000003`) tracks economy-wide *average* hourly earnings for all private nonfarm employees. Your individual paycheck depends heavily on your specific industry, job role, geographic region, hours worked, and individual career moves, which can deviate significantly from the national average.
